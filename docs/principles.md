# Methodological principles

## Situated rather than civilizational

The ontology does not posit a coherent entity called “Western music” against a set of
“other musics.” Concepts are situated by language, period, repertoire, locality,
institution, instrument, medium, and practice. Dominance is scoped: a system may be
dominant in software, conservatory pedagogy, a liturgy, an ensemble, or a recording
industry without being universally dominant.

## Relational rather than taxonomic

A rāga need not be made a subtype of MODE. A makam, maqām, pathet, rāga, and dastgāh
can partially overlap or illuminate aspects of one another while retaining distinct
histories and internal vocabularies.

## Synthetic, emergent, and hybrid

Pitch organizations may arise through embodied practice, explicit theoretical
construction, instrument making, institutional standardization, or combinations of
these. These are processes, not permanent mutually exclusive classes.

## Knowledge provenance

The ontology distinguishes:
- locally named/verbalized categories
- demonstrated or tacit practice
- participant judgment
- experimentally inferred organization
- analyst-derived models
- historical textual reconstruction
- measured acoustical observations
- institutional standards
- computational abstractions

Absence of a local lexical equivalent is evidence, not a missing database field.

## Cross-explanation

`ILLUMINATES_ASPECT_OF` is intentionally non-genealogical. Svara may help someone
understand a property of perde; sléndro may help destabilize fixed-frequency assumptions
about another system; Aka tuning research may expose assumptions hidden in the word
“scale.” Such bridges must state both the useful analogy and its limit.

## Influence

Influence should be qualified where possible: conquest, trade, migration, diaspora,
religious transmission, court patronage, colonial schooling, scholarship, instrument
diffusion, recording, broadcasting, software, or institutional standardization.

## No compulsory cents

A cents representation may describe a measurement or model. It is never required as
proof that a pitch concept is well formed.
