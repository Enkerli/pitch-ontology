# Pitch and time in one ontology

The temporal records were added after the pitch records, and the question of how they sit
together is not settled by putting them in the same repository.

## Why the same repository

Three reasons, in descending order of strength.

**The same concepts recur.** A centre established by return rather than by ordinal
position appears as a tonic, a karār, a final — and as sam, a gong stroke, a clave
orientation. A representational technology that fixes a grid and then treats departures
from it as error appears as equal temperament and as the beat grid. A travelling system
that arrives with a notation and displaces local vocabulary appears as the staff and as
4/4. These are not analogies drawn for effect; they are the same argument about the same
kind of object.

**The same documents cover both.** Keil's participatory discrepancies are explicitly
about timing *and* tuning. Toussaint applies one geometric apparatus to scales and
rhythms. Jacoby and colleagues ask about priors on pitch and on rhythm with the same
method. Splitting the repository would split these records.

**The same institutions decide both.** MIDI fixes twelve semitones per octave and
twenty-four ticks per quarter note in the same specification. A DAW ships a chromatic
scale menu and a 4/4 grid as defaults on the same screen. The pitch and time defaults of
music software have one history.

## Why it is not a mirror

The temporal domains are deliberately not a reflection of the pitch domains.
`temporal_organization` sits where `pitch_resources` does and `metric_organization` where
`modal_organization` does, but `ensemble_coordination` has no pitch counterpart, and that
asymmetry is real rather than an oversight.

Rhythm raises the relational problem harder. `ensemble_interlocking` describes an
organization in which no part states the pattern and each is incomplete alone;
`clave_direction` is a property that exists only relative to other parts and to a dance.
Pitch has relational concepts, but it is much easier to write a defensible pitch record
about a single sounding line than a defensible rhythm record about one. A representation
that stores a single stream loses more in time than it does in pitch.

The opposite asymmetry also holds. Pitch has a far richer apparatus of measurement units
and interchange formats — cents, ratios, the Scala format, MIDI tuning — because pitch was
standardized earlier and more completely. Time has PPQN and a swing percentage.

## The equivalent of "these are not modes"

On the pitch side, the recurring error is translating a locally named category into
**mode** or **scale**, and the ontology's cautions repeat that this is lossy in a
particular direction.

The temporal equivalent is **metre**, and the record for it says so. Tāl is not a metre,
uṣūl is not a time signature, a timeline is not a bar, irama is not tempo, laya is not
tempo. Each is translated into the metric apparatus on first contact, and what survives is
the count.

The pairing is exact enough to be useful: makam sits beside usûl, maqām beside īqāʿ, and
in both traditions the two are taught together. An ontology that held the pitch member of
each pair and not the temporal one would be describing half of a curriculum.

## Where the seam actually is

`maximally_even_set` is the formal object that describes a diatonic set and a bell pattern
with one definition, and it is the concept in this ontology most likely to be misused. The
identity is in the representation — points on a circle — and both applications require the
circle to be equally divided first. Equal division of the octave and equal division of a
cycle are each historical achievements with contested records here.

The verified citation network measures how little the two literatures talk. Of the
citations checked against real reference lists, exactly one runs between a pitch record and
a rhythm record, and it runs inside a single research group citing itself. The
`BRIDGES_FIELD_TO` edges across that seam are curatorial because there is nothing to
verify — which is the finding, not a gap in the method.
