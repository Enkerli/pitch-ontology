# Scope: pitch is not equally central everywhere

An ontology of pitch has a built-in temptation: to treat pitch as the dimension through
which musicking is organized, and everything else as decoration around it. This page
states the opposite commitment, and `pitch_centrality` records it inside the graph so it
can be reasoned about rather than merely believed.

## Uneven focus

Pitch and scale get very different amounts of attention across practices, traditions and
discourses.

- Some musics are, in the ordinary sense of the word, **obsessed with tonality** —
  including a great deal of post-tonal work, whose departures remain organized by what
  they depart from.
- Some bodies of theory tie pitch to **acoustics**, most persistently through the
  overtone series, which is then reinterpreted in ways specific to each theory that
  invokes it. The reinterpretations are data about the theories, not noise.
- In other musics, notes matter less, or are reinterpreted through **timbre, rhythm,
  text, gesture, instrument construction, or social arrangement** — dimensions in which
  pitch participates without governing.

None of these is the default case. A practice with little elaborated pitch discourse is
not a practice whose participants hear pitch less finely, and elaborate pitch theory can
coexist with performance where players attend mainly to something else. Discourse and
practice can differ in what they foreground, and both are describable.

## The apparatus has its own bias

Fundamental frequency is cheap to extract. Timbre, gesture, and text-setting are
expensive to describe and harder to compare across recordings. Corpus and
signal-processing tools therefore produce pitch-organized accounts faster than practices
would warrant, and comparative work inherits that ratio.

This repository is one of those instruments. The bias is recorded as a claim
(`description-tooling-raises-pitch-centrality`) rather than left in prose, so that
anything the ontology asserts about pitch organization can be read against the fact that
a pitch ontology was doing the asserting.

## What this is for

This is preliminary work, done with curiosity. It is not an attempt to reach a unified
theory of music through F0, and it does not need one to be useful. The aim is to hold
situated concepts next to each other with their differences intact — to make partial
overlaps, contested translations and historical shifts legible — so that connections
become visible without being collapsed into identity.

Where a concept turns out to be marginal to the practice it came from, that is a finding
worth recording, not a gap to be filled.
